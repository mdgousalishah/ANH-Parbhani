About these records

Legal importance

These records confirm the original Waqf endowment of the Masjid land. They are essential evidence for protecting the property from illegal encroachment and preserving the institution's heritage under the Waqf Act.

This historic document from the Nizam era is the cornerstone of our institution's history and legal standing. It details the original service endowment, formally known as Khidmat-e-Mash, of more than 13 acres of land granted exclusively for the establishment and continuous upkeep of the Masjid e Mehtab Ali Shah complex and the Ashoorkhana Naale Hyder.

The grant was made as a sacred trust, so that the complex would remain a centre for prayer, community gathering and spiritual remembrance for generations to come. The document sets out the survey numbers and boundaries of the land, underlining its religious significance and its inalienable status as Waqf property.

"This endowment was not merely a gift of land, but a divine commitment to the future of our community: a promise that this sacred space would be protected, maintained and preserved until the end of time."

As the institution faces modern challenges, this document matters more than ever. It is the primary reference in our legal and administrative efforts against illegal encroachment. Preserving this land is not only about protecting property; it is a religious obligation to honour the intentions of our founders and safeguard our spiritual heritage.

Chronology of the endowment

  1. 1880

    Original endowment issued

    The Sanad is granted by the Nizam Government, establishing the Khidmat-e-Mash.

  2. 1961

    Trust registration

    Registered under the Bombay Public Trust Act, securing its legal status in modern India.

  3. 1987

    Administrative scheme

    An official legal scheme for the administration and management of the trust is adopted.

  4. Present day

    Active protection

    The Mutawalli and the Waqf Board use these documents to reclaim encroached land.
