The Foundation

A Royal Endowment (Inaam)

The history of this sacred complex begins with a generous "Inaam" (Grant) from the 6th Nizam of Hyderabad, H.H. Sir Mir Mahboob Ali Khan.

The original Sanad (Deed) dates back to 08-08-1885 (Shawwal 1302 Hijri) and bears the official seal of the Nizam's government. This grant established the land as a "Waqf by dedication"—a permanent endowment dedicated to God. The administration was entrusted to the first Mutawalli, Haji Syed Mahemood Ali Shah, marking the beginning of a long lineage of caretakers.

Milestones of Stewardship

The Silsila (Chain) of our caretakers and legal milestones.

08 August 1885 (1302 Hijri)

Creation of Waqf

The land was granted by Nizam VI to Haji Syed Mahemood Ali Shah (First Mutawalli), establishing the Ashoorkhana and Musafirkhana for spiritual gatherings.

September 26, 1961

Official Trust Registration

The trust was officially registered under the Bombay Public Trust Act with the name 'Shri. Jumma Masjid Amadypur' (Reg No: B.IS 123/61).

January 27, 1987

Administrative Scheme Adopted

A formal scheme for administration was legally adopted to ensure transparent governance. Key figures in the lineage during this era included Syed Akbar Ali Shah and Syed Anwar Ali Shah.

Present Day

Modern Leadership

Under the guidance of the current Mutawalli, Mohammed Mushtaque Ahmed, the trust focuses on digitization (UMEED Reg: UCP0003), education, and preserving the Waqf land against encroachment.

Architectural Heritage

Ashoorkhana Naale Hyder

The spiritual core of the complex, built in the distinguished Qutub Shahi style with characteristic arches and minarets. It serves as a sacred space for mourning and remembrance during Muharram, echoing centuries of devotion.

Naqqar Khana (Drum House)

Situated at the entrance, this structure was traditionally used to play drums (Naubat) to announce prayer times and special occasions, a hallmark of Nizam-era town planning.

The Baradari & Musafirkhana

The Baradari is a twelve-doored pavilion designed for cross-ventilation, used for community assemblies. Adjacent is the Musafirkhana, a guest house built to provide shelter for travelers and pilgrims, reflecting the Islamic virtue of hospitality.
